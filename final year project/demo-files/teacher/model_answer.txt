QUESTION 1 (5 Marks):
"Explain the process of photosynthesis in detail including the raw materials,
products, and factors affecting the rate of photosynthesis."

MODEL ANSWER:

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants manufacture their own food
using sunlight energy, carbon dioxide, and water, in the presence of chlorophyll.

CHEMICAL EQUATION:
6CO₂ + 6H₂O  →(Sunlight/Chlorophyll)→  C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
(Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen)

RAW MATERIALS (Inputs):
1. Carbon dioxide (CO₂): Absorbed from air through stomata (small pores in leaves)
2. Water (H₂O): Absorbed by roots from soil and transported through xylem
3. Sunlight: Radiant energy absorbed by chlorophyll pigment
4. Chlorophyll: Green pigment present in chloroplasts of plant cells — captures light energy

PRODUCTS (Outputs):
1. Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆): Food/energy stored for the plant
2. Oxygen (O₂): Released as a by-product through stomata

WHERE IT OCCURS: Inside chloroplasts, specifically on the thylakoid membranes (light reactions)
and in the stroma (dark reactions/Calvin cycle)

FACTORS AFFECTING PHOTOSYNTHESIS:
1. Light Intensity: Higher light → more photosynthesis (up to saturation point)
2. CO₂ Concentration: More CO₂ → faster rate (up to a limit)
3. Temperature: Optimal 25-35°C; too high destroys enzymes, too low slows reactions
4. Water Availability: Lack of water causes stomata to close, reducing CO₂ intake
5. Chlorophyll content: More chlorophyll → more light absorption

- Chemical equation (correct): 1 mark
- Raw materials (minimum 3): 1 mark
- Products (both): 1 mark
- Factors (minimum 3): 1.5 marks
- Clarity and completeness: 0.5 marks

QUESTION 2 (5 Marks):
"Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic respiration with examples."

MODEL ANSWER:

AEROBIC RESPIRATION:
- Definition: Breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen to produce energy
- Equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + 38 ATP (energy)
- Location: Mitochondria (complete oxidation)
- Products: Carbon dioxide, water, energy (ATP)
- Energy yield: 38 ATP molecules per glucose molecule
- Efficiency: High energy yield
- Example: Occurs in most living organisms including humans, animals, and plants
  (when we run/exercise, muscles use aerobic respiration)

ANAEROBIC RESPIRATION:
- Definition: Breakdown of glucose without oxygen (partial oxidation)
- Equations:
  * In yeast/fungi: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + 2 ATP (fermentation)
    (glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy)
  * In muscles (during heavy exercise): C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ + 2 ATP
    (glucose → lactic acid + energy) — causes muscle cramps
- Location: Cytoplasm
- Products: Ethanol + CO₂ (yeast) OR Lactic acid (muscle cells)
- Energy yield: Only 2 ATP molecules per glucose
- Efficiency: Low energy yield

KEY DIFFERENCES TABLE:
| Feature        | Aerobic               | Anaerobic              |
|---------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Oxygen        | Required              | Not required           |
| Products      | CO₂ + H₂O + ATP      | Ethanol/Lactic acid+ATP|
| Energy        | 38 ATP                | 2 ATP                  |
| Location      | Mitochondria          | Cytoplasm              |
| Glucose       | Completely oxidized   | Partially oxidized     |
| Examples      | Humans, animals       | Yeast, muscle cramps   |

REAL-LIFE APPLICATIONS:
- Yeast fermentation: used in making bread, beer, wine
- Lactic acid fermentation: curd/yogurt making
- Muscle fatigue during intense exercise

- Anaerobic definition + equations (both): 1.5 marks
- 4 correct differences: 1.5 marks

QUESTION 3 (3 Marks):
"What is the role of stomata in plants?"

MODEL ANSWER:

Stomata are tiny pores (openings) present mainly on the lower surface of leaves.
They are surrounded by specialized guard cells that control their opening and closing.

ROLES OF STOMATA:
1. Gas Exchange: Allow CO₂ to enter for photosynthesis and O₂ to exit
2. Transpiration: Release of excess water vapor from leaves into the atmosphere
   - This creates a pull (transpiration pull) that helps in upward movement of water
3. Regulation: Guard cells open stomata in light (when CO₂ is needed) and close
   in darkness or drought to prevent water loss

MECHANISM OF OPENING AND CLOSING:
- In light: Guard cells absorb water by osmosis → become turgid → stomata OPEN
- In darkness/drought: Guard cells lose water → become flaccid → stomata CLOSE

SIGNIFICANCE: Without stomata, photosynthesis cannot occur as CO₂ cannot enter
the leaf. Over 90% of water lost by plants is through stomata (transpiration).
